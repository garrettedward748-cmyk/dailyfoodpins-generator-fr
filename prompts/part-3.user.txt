notes_html
- Each section is an <h2> followed by one <p> of 40 to 60 words. Nothing else
  between them.
- Use the source's serving suggestions and storage notes. Where the source
  is silent, say what the technique implies, not what would be nice.
- Storage advice must be food-safe and put each time in the right place:
  cooked food keeps 2 to 4 days in the fridge, and weeks to a few months in
  the freezer. Never "freezer for a few days", and never leave cooked meat,
  fish, eggs or dairy (yoghurt sauces included) at room temperature for more
  than 2 hours.
- Format: <h2>Avec quoi servir ce plat</h2><p>…</p><h2>À préparer à l'avance</h2><p>…</p>
  Headings are in French, sentence case, no final full stop.

faqs
- Objects of the shape {"question": "", "answer_html": ""}, as many as THE
  SHAPE above says.
- Questions a cook asks before starting or halfway through, in their own
  words: a substitution, a step that looks wrong, keeping or freezing it.
- Never ask a question the steps already answer plainly.
- When the recipe JSON has "google_questions" (what people really ask Google
  about this dish), take the questions from there, worded as they are.
- answer_html: one <p>, 25 to 50 words, a direct answer first and the reason
  second. Say no when the answer is no.

nutrition
- Per serving. Whole numbers for calories and sodium_mg; one decimal is fine
  for the gram fields.
- Take the source's figures where it has them and estimate the rest from the
  ingredients and the serving count. Use 0 only for a field that is genuinely
  zero.

tags
- 4 to 7 short lowercase French tags: the method, the diet, the occasion, the main
  ingredient. « végétarien », « one pot », « ramadan », « semoule », « à préparer à l'avance ».
- No tag that repeats the category or the cuisine. No hashtags.
